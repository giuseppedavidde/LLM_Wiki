## The Tension

Within the crypto_trading domain itself, two companion articles stake opposite claims
about what drives cryptocurrency prices. Crypto Technical Analysis asserts that crypto
is the asset class where TA works *best* — retail-driven, 24/7, high volatility,
psychological round numbers. Crypto Fundamental Analysis asserts that intrinsic value
is knowable through on-chain metrics, tokenomics, and team quality, and is best for
*long* horizons while TA peaks over *short* periods. Each claims the more reliable edge.

## Position A — Technical Analysis Works Best for Crypto

The TA article argues crypto's structural features make charts *more* reliable than in
equities: "Retail-driven sentiment... creates recognizable, repeating chart
formations"; "24/7 markets... no gaps between sessions means continuous price data";
"high volatility... clearer breakout signals." Specific patterns get quantified
confidence — "Head and Shoulders (~85% accuracy)," "most common reversal formations."
Volume is "the most critical confirming indicator": a breakout on low volume "is
likely a head fake." The implicit thesis: because crypto is *less* efficient (more
retail, round-number fixation), historical patterns repeat with exploitable
regularity.

## Position B — Fundamental Analysis Determines Value

The FA article counters with intrinsic-value lens: on-chain activity (active addresses,
hash rate, exchange flows), token economics (fixed vs unlimited supply, burning,
halving), valuation metrics (NVT, Stock-to-Flow, market cap dominance), and team/
development diligence. The decisive claim: **"FA is best paired with TA — use FA to
identify what to buy and TA to determine when to buy,"** and **"FA effectiveness
increases over longer time horizons; TA effectiveness peaks over shorter periods."**
FA treats the chart-pattern reliability figures as short-horizon noise around a
fundamental trajectory the chain itself reveals.

## Resolution / Synthesis

The articles themselves supply the synthesis: a **horizon-dependent division of
labour**. FA selects (which projects have on-chain adoption and sound tokenomics); TA
times (when to enter/exit on the chart). The contradiction sharpens at the extremes.
Pure-TA crypto traders implicitly assume the market is *inefficient enough* to keep
repeating patterns — an assumption FA questions at long horizons, where NVT or
Stock-to-Flow deviations from chart patterns eventually reassert. Pure-FA traders
ignore that "events weeks or months out are often not priced in" precisely because the
retail-driven inefficiency TA exploits exists. The irreducible tension: crypto may be
*simultaneously* inefficient on short patterns (TA edge) and *eventually* mean-reverting
to on-chain value (FA edge). Neither fully invalidates the other; each fails at the
other's timescale.
