## The Tension

The crypto ecosystem hosts two irreconcilable philosophies about where the edge lives.
The hype/momentum school buys *because* price and chatter are rising — momentum is the
signal, narrative is the fuel, and exit comes when the crowd fatigues. The fundamental
value school buys *because* the network has real utility, adoption, scarcity, or cash-flow
that the current price underprices — narrative is noise and price is expected to revert to
underlying value. One trades the mania; the other refuses to participate in it.

## Position A — Hype / Momentum (Crypto Hype Analysis)

The crypto-hype framework treats attention as the primary asset and FOMO as a tradable
phase. Pump detection scores hype level and squeeze potential from social volume and price
velocity; the entry is tilted toward the **acceleration** leg, not the discounted entry.
Technical analysis is applied opportunistically: support/resistance and breakouts on the
5-minute chart for **binary-event plays** (listings, halvings, ETF approvals), exploiting
the volatility these events inject. Richmond's *Crypto Crash Course* acknowledges the
gambling character of the space and reconceives the speculator's job as managing
*casino-style* risk per trade rather than naming intrinsic value. The edge is in the
crowd's *behavior*, not the asset's *worth*.

## Position B — Fundamental Value (Crypto Fundamental Analysis)

The fundamental school reads the whitepaper, on-chain throughput, tokenomics, dev
activity, and network security before transacting. Alan John & Jon Law's *Crypto
Technical Analysis* repeatedly pairs fundamentals with the chart precisely "because price
moves on hype, but fundamentals whoregate the destination." The crash-course ecosystem
treats blockchain-technology, decentralization, and scarcity as **long-horizon value
drivers**: the dip-buyer accumulates the surviving protocol *below* its network value and
waits years, not minutes. Price disconnected from fundamentals is a **speculative bubble
to be avoided**, not a momentum trend to ride.

## Resolution / Synthesis

The two are **time-horizon reconcilable but philosophically hostile**. Fundamentals set
the *universe* — which protocols survive long enough to matter; hype sets the *timing* —
which of them will move in the next session. A trader can hold fundamentally-selected
core positions while trading hype momentum on the satellite book, accepting that the two
require different risk regimes (small per-trade size on hype, large conviction on value).
The irreducible residue is ethics and drawdown tolerance: hype trading survives being
wrong about *value* but not being wrong about *timing*; value investing survives being
wrong about *timing* but not about *which protocol endures*. The crash-course framing —
"casino, manage the bet" — is the honest bridge: treat hype as a gamble whose odds depend
on the underlying network's survival probability rather than as an investment.
