# Wyckoff Method — Modern Adaptation

> Sources: David Weis, 2013; Rubén Villahermosa Chaves, 2021; Anna Coulling, 2013
> Raw: [Trades About to Happen](../../raw/trading/2026-05-20-trades-about-to-happen.md); [Wyckoff 2.0 Structures](../../raw/trading/2026-05-20-wyckoff-2-structures.md); [A Complete Guide To Volume Price Analysis](../../raw/trading/2026-05-20-volume-price-analysis.md)

## Overview

The Wyckoff Method, originally developed by Richard Wyckoff in the early 20th century, provides a comprehensive framework for understanding market behavior through the lens of supply and demand. Three modern adaptations are covered here:
- **David Weis** (Trades About to Happen) — shifts emphasis from accumulation/distribution labeling to finding trades through price/volume behavior framed by lines
- **Rubén Villahermosa Chaves** (Wyckoff 2.0) — integrates classical Wyckoff with Volume Profile and Order Flow
- **Anna Coulling** (A Complete Guide To Volume Price Analysis) — distills VPA into a streamlined, practical system based on the confirmation/anomaly principle, using candlesticks instead of bars

The approach is applicable across all markets (stocks, futures, forex, crypto) and all timeframes (intraday to monthly).

## Core Principles

Weis builds on Wyckoff's foundation with these key analytical tools:

1. **Effort vs. Reward** — Compare the volume (effort) with the resulting price movement (reward). Large volume with little price progress indicates a potential turning point.
2. **Ease of Movement** — Wide price bars show ease of movement in that direction; narrow bars show difficulty.
3. **Position of the Close** — Where the close falls within a bar's range reveals who is in control (close near high = buyers dominant; near low = sellers dominant).
4. **Shortening of Thrust (SOT)** — When successive price swings in the trend direction become shorter, momentum is weakening. SOT can apply to both price range and volume.
5. **Follow-Through** — After support/resistance penetrations, the presence or absence of follow-through determines whether the breakout is real or a trap (spring/upthrust).
6. **Tests of High-Volume Areas** — Price often returns to previous high-volume areas to test the strength of supply or demand.
7. **Interaction with Trend Lines** — Support/resistance lines, trend lines, and channels frame the price/volume story.

## The Three Laws of Wyckoff

1. **Law of Supply and Demand** — When demand exceeds supply, prices rise; when supply exceeds demand, prices fall. This is the engine of all market movement.
2. **Law of Cause and Effect** — Accumulation (cause) leads to an uptrend (effect); distribution (cause) leads to a downtrend (effect). The duration and extent of the cause determine the magnitude of the effect.
3. **Law of Effort vs. Result** — Divergences between volume (effort) and price (result) signal potential turning points.

## Advanced Concepts (Villahermosa)

### Structural Failure
- **Weakness** — price fails to hold a support break and reverses
- **Strength** — price fails to hold a resistance break and reverses

### Accumulation vs. Failed Distribution
A key analytical challenge: distinguishing genuine accumulation from a failed distribution. The context and subsequent price/volume behavior determine which is occurring.

### Sloping Structures
Not all trading ranges are horizontal — structures can slope upward or downward while still functioning as accumulation or distribution zones.

### The Labels
Wyckoff schemas use standard labels (SC, AR, ST, LPS, UT, etc.) to identify phases within accumulation and distribution. These labels are guidelines, not rigid rules — the market must be given flexibility.

## The "Story of the Lines"

Lines drawn on charts (support, resistance, trend lines, channels) interconnect to tell a story. They bring price movement into focus and guide the trader toward behavior that prompts action. Rather than mechanically following breakout signals, the trader reads the full context of how price interacts with previously drawn lines.

## Volume Price Analysis — Coulling Adaptation

Anna Coulling's VPA is the most streamlined of the three modern adaptations. It distills the Wyckoff heritage into a single principle: **confirmation vs. anomaly**. Every price bar is evaluated for whether volume confirms the price move or reveals a divergence.

Key differences from Weis and Villahermosa:

| Aspect | Weis | Villahermosa | Coulling |
|--------|------|-------------|----------|
| Chart type | Bars | Candles | Candles |
| Core tool | Weis Wave, lines | Volume Profile, Order Flow | VPA confirmation/anomaly |
| Complexity | Medium | High | Low |
| Entry method | Springs/upthrusts | Profile + footprint + Wyckoff | Premier candles + volume |

Coulling's VPA is particularly effective for traders who want a volume-based approach without the full Wyckoff schema. All three adaptions agree on the fundamental premise: volume reveals the truth behind price movement.
