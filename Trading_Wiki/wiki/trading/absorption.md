# Absorption

> Sources: David Weis, 2013; Rubén Villahermosa Chaves, 2021
> Raw: [Trades About to Happen](../../raw/trading/2026-05-20-trades-about-to-happen.md); [Wyckoff 2.0 Structures](../../raw/trading/2026-05-20-wyckoff-2-structures.md)

## Overview

Absorption is the process through which the market overcomes opposing orders — long liquidation, profit taking, and new short selling. It represents the struggle at potential turning points where one side's orders must be fully absorbed before the trend can continue or reverse.

## Absorption at Tops

When sellers (longs liquidating, profit-takers, new shorts) meet ongoing buying demand. Clues that absorption is succeeding (buyers winning):

1. **Rising supports** — each pullback finds buyers at a higher level
2. **Volume increases around the top** of the absorption area, showing active buying absorbing supply
3. **Lack of downward follow-through** after threatening price bars — price dips fail to deepen
4. **Prices press against the resistance line** without giving ground, showing persistent demand
5. **Minor upthrusts during absorption** fail to produce a breakdown
6. **Absorption may be resolved by a spring** — a false breakdown that reverses sharply

## Absorption at Bottoms

When sellers overcome buying (short covering, short liquidation, bottom-picking by new longs). The main characteristic: repeated inability of prices to rally away from the low. Persistently heavy volume hammering against the low signals an imminent breakdown.

## Wyckoff's "Bag-Holding"

When persistent selling fails to produce further weakness at a low, large operators are trapping shorts. This is a form of absorption by sellers that precedes a rally — the selling is being absorbed by hidden buying.

## Absorption vs. Distribution

Weis focuses on the dynamics of absorption rather than trying to determine whether accumulation or distribution is occurring. The price/volume clues reveal the immediate balance of power regardless of the broader phase.

## Absorption and Auction Market Theory

Absorption aligns closely with Auction Market Theory: when price reaches a High Volume Node (HVN), the market is in a zone of acceptance. Absorption occurs when one side must fully absorb the opposing orders before price can exit the zone.

### Buying Absorption (Bearish Turn)
Large volume on the BID (passive buying) at resistance → Initiative Selling attacks the BID → downward reversal

### Selling Absorption (Bullish Turn)
Large volume on the ASK (passive selling) at support → Initiative Buying attacks the ASK → upward reversal

These Order Flow patterns provide the micro-structure confirmation of absorption that Weis describes at the bar chart level.
