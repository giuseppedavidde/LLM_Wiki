# Volume Profile

> Sources: Rubén Villahermosa Chaves, 2021; Trader Dale, Unknown
> Raw: [Wyckoff 2.0 Structures](../../raw/trading/2026-05-20-wyckoff-2-structures.md); [Volume Profile: The Insiders Guide](../../raw/trading/2026-05-20-volume-profile-trader-dale.md)

## Overview

Volume Profile is a variant of Market Profile (developed by J. Peter Steidlmayer in 1985 for the CBOT). Unlike Order Flow analysis, Volume Profile is totally objective — it does not require interpretation. It provides a horizontal histogram showing the number of contracts traded at each price level, identifying zones of greater and lesser interest.

## Core Principles

- The market has a **memory** and tends to repeat behavior
- Areas where high volume occurred are likely to act as support/resistance in the future
- **Market memory is primarily short-term** — more recent zones are more important than older ones
- If price has been away from an acceptance area for a long time, it becomes less significant

## Volume Profile Composition

Volume is displayed as a horizontal histogram distributed by price level. The more contracts traded at a level, the longer the histogram bar. Key components:

- **Point of Control (POC)** — the price level with the highest traded volume
- **Value Area (VA)** — the range of prices where a specified percentage (typically 70%) of volume occurred
- **Value Area High (VAH)** — top of the value area
- **Value Area Low (VAL)** — bottom of the value area
- **High Volume Node (HVN)** — price level with high volume (acceptance)
- **Low Volume Node (LVN)** — price level with low volume (potential rejection zone)

## Types of Profiles

Profiles can be categorized by their shape, which reveals market behavior:

### Villahermosa Classification
- **P-shape** — normal distribution, balanced market
- **b-shape** — two high-volume areas, potential double distribution

### Trader Dale Classification
- **D-profile** — a single high-volume node near the middle of the range with declining volume toward the edges. Indicates a balanced, two-sided market. The POC is the most significant area, usually near the profile center.
- **P-profile** — a single high-volume node skewed to one side. Indicates that price found acceptance at one extreme with thin volume on the opposite side. Most significant when the market is in an uptrend (bullish) or at the possible end of a downtrend.
- **b-profile** — two distinct high-volume nodes (double distribution). Indicates a shift in value. Most significant in a downtrend (bearish) or at the possible end of an uptrend.
- **Thin profile** — low volume across a wide price range. Indicates a lack of acceptance; price moved through quickly. Often occurs at session open or after news events. Can signal potential reversal zones.

## Vertical vs. Horizontal Volume

- **Vertical volume** — standard volume bars shown below price (time-based)
- **Horizontal volume** — Volume Profile shown at each price level (price-based)

Volume Profile provides information that vertical volume cannot: it shows *where* volume occurred at each price level, not just *how much* occurred in a time period.

## Difference from Market Profile

Market Profile uses letters to represent time periods. Volume Profile uses actual volume data. Volume Profile is more precise and objective, while Market Profile emphasizes time-based TPO (Time Price Opportunity).

## Uses of Volume Profile

1. **Identifying structures** — HVNs and LVNs reveal where structures are forming
2. **Determining market bias** — where price is relative to the value area indicates bullish or bearish bias
3. **Analyzing trend health** — healthy trends show price moving away from value with acceptance
4. **VPOC Migration** — watching the POC move from one level to another reveals shifting value
5. **Calibration of position management** — value areas guide stop placement and profit targets

## Trading Principles with Value Areas

| Principle | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Trading Range** | When price is within a well-defined value area, trade mean reversion |
| **Reversion** | When price moves excessively away from value, expect a return |
| **Continuation** | When price breaks from value with acceptance, trend in that direction |
| **Failed Reversion** | When price attempts to return to value but fails, trend continuation |

## Practical Volume Setups (Trader Dale)

### Volume Accumulation Setup
Identifies areas where institutions are building positions. Look for a sideways price area with above-average volume at a key support level. The setup is confirmed when price breaks above the accumulation zone on expanding volume, signalling the start of a new trend.

### Trend Setup
Designed to catch strong directional moves. Once a trend is confirmed by price action (aggressive initiation candles), the Volume Profile shows where value is being built. Enter on pullbacks to the POC or value area edge when volume confirms the trend direction.

### Rejection Setup
Identifies areas where price was strongly rejected (significant LVN created by a single large-volume bar). Price returning to this zone on declining volume confirms the rejection is still valid. Used for mean-reversion entries at extreme levels.

### Reversal Trades
A special case of the rejection setup where price breaks a key level then immediately reverses, creating a large-volume rejection candle. The profile of the rejection session reveals the new value area forming in the opposite direction.
