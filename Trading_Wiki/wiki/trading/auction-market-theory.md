# Auction Market Theory

## Overview

Auction Market Theory (AMT) provides the theoretical foundation for understanding how markets function as auction processes. Developed through the work of J. Peter Steidlmayer and others, AMT explains that markets are not random but follow a repetitive auction process of balancing and imbalance between buyers and sellers.

## Core Variables

AMT operates on three variables:
- **Price** — the current value at which transactions occur
- **Volume** — the number of contracts/shares traded, representing participation
- **Time** — the duration spent at each price level, indicating acceptance or rejection

## Perception of Value

The market constantly seeks to define "value" — the price level where the most trading occurs. When price moves away from the value zone, it is expected to return (reversion). This perception of value is dynamic and changes as new information enters the market.

## The Four Steps of Market Activity

1. **Accumulation/Distribution** — price moves into balance after a period of imbalance
2. **Trend** — directional price movement driven by供求 imbalance
3. **Climax** — explosive activity that exhausts the trend
4. **Reversion** — return to value after a climax

## The Law of Supply and Demand

The true engine of financial markets. Regardless of participant type or intent, every transaction represents supply meeting demand. The relationship between BID (buying) and ASK (selling) determines price movement:
- **Excess demand** → price rises
- **Excess supply** → price falls
- **Balance** → price moves sideways (trading range)

## Common Errors in AMT Interpretation

- Confusing short-term noise with structural shifts
- Ignoring the difference between passive and active orders
- Over-interpreting small imbalances without context

## Bid/Ask, Spread and Liquidity

- **BID** — the highest price a buyer is willing to pay
- **ASK** — the lowest price a seller is willing to accept
- **Spread** — the difference between BID and ASK
- **Liquidity** — the ability to execute orders without significant price impact
