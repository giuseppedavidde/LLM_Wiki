# Volume Price Analysis (VPA)

> Sources: Anna Coulling, 2013
> Raw: [A Complete Guide To Volume Price Analysis](../../raw/trading/2026-05-20-volume-price-analysis.md)

## Overview

Volume Price Analysis (VPA) is a methodology that uses volume to validate or invalidate price action. Developed by Anna Coulling, it is a simplified, practical approach rooted in the same principles used by Charles Dow, Jesse Livermore, and Richard Wyckoff. VPA focuses on a single question: does the volume confirm the price move, or does it reveal an anomaly?

VPA applies to all markets (stocks, forex, futures, crypto) and all timeframes. It requires no indicators — only price and volume.

## Why Volume Is the Only Leading Indicator

All conventional indicators (RSI, MACD, moving averages) are lagging — they compute from past price data. Volume is the only real-time measure of participation. It reveals:

- Whether a price move is genuine or false
- When the "smart money" (insiders, institutions) is entering or exiting
- When a trend is likely to continue or reverse

Volume cannot be hidden. Every transaction is recorded. The challenge is learning to read what volume says about the intentions behind the price move.

## The Right Price

Price alone tells only half the story. It shows where buying and selling occurred, but not the conviction behind the move. A breakout to a new high on low volume means something entirely different from a breakout on high volume. Price without volume is a narrative without evidence.

### Key Insight: Fake Moves

The most important function of volume is distinguishing genuine moves from traps. A price breakout on declining volume is suspect. A breakdown on low volume that reverses sharply is a potential spring (or "false break" in Coulling's terminology).

## VPA First Principles

### Confirmation vs. Anomaly

VPA reduces to one binary judgment for every price bar:

- **Confirmation** — volume and price agree (rising price + rising volume = strong trend; falling price + rising volume = strong selling)
- **Anomaly** — volume and price disagree (rising price + falling volume = weak rally; falling price + falling volume = selling exhaustion)

The anomaly is always the warning signal — the first sign that the current move is losing conviction.

### Guiding Principles

1. **Art, Not Science** — VPA requires subjective judgment. Software cannot replicate the contextual analysis of comparing price behaviour against volume history.
2. **Patience** — Markets are like supertankers; they do not reverse instantly. A signal candle is just the first clue — wait for confirmation from subsequent price action.
3. **Volume Validates Price** — Always start with the candle/bar, then look for validation or anomaly in the volume bar.

### The Three-Step Process

1. **Micro (single candle)** — analyse the price/volume relationship of one bar
2. **Macro (small groups)** — compare adjacent candles and their volume for patterns
3. **Global (cycles)** — identify the broader accumulation/distribution cycle

## Accumulation and Distribution

Markets move in cycles driven by insider/smart money activity:

- **Accumulation** — the smart money buys quietly while the public sells in fear. Characterised by high volume on down moves that fail to follow through, followed by low-volume pullbacks.
- **Distribution** — the smart money sells quietly while the public buys in greed. High volume on up moves that stall, followed by low-volume rallies that fail to make new highs.
- **Climax (the "firework display")** — the end of a cycle. A massive volume spike that exhausts the trend. Buying climax at tops, selling climax at bottoms.

The three-step process above maps to this: micro analysis catches individual accumulation/distribution candles, macro reveals the pattern forming, and global confirms the cycle phase.

## The Three Premier Candles

Coulling identifies three candles as the most significant for VPA:

### Shooting Star (Weakness)

Price opens, rallies, then closes near the open with a long upper wick. Signals potential weakness. The significance depends entirely on volume:

- Below-average volume → possible pause, minor pullback
- High volume → significant resistance, potential reversal
- Above-average volume at a trend high → strong warning of distribution

### Hammer (Strength)

Price opens, sells off, then recovers to close near the open with a long lower wick. Signals potential strength. Volume determines conviction:

- Low volume → minor support test
- High volume at a trend low → strong accumulation signal
- Confirmation requires follow-through in the next candle

### Doji (Indecision)

Open and close are nearly equal. Represents complete balance between buyers and sellers. On its own meaningless; with volume it becomes significant:

- High-volume doji at a trend extreme → potential reversal zone
- Low-volume doji in a range — continuation of indecision

## Stopping Volume

Price action: strength in a downtrend.

When a downtrend is in full force, the insiders apply "the brakes" by buying against the selling pressure. Stopping volume appears as:

- Deep lower wicks over 2–3 candles (buying absorbing the sell-off)
- Candle closes in the upper half of the range
- Volume increasing on each successive down candle being absorbed

The pattern shows sellers exhausting as buyers absorb supply. Stopping volume precedes a selling climax and the eventual reversal.

## Topping Out Volume

Price action: weakness in an uptrend.

The mirror of stopping volume. As an uptrend matures, insiders begin distributing. Topping out volume appears as:

- Deep upper wicks over 2–3 candles (selling absorbing the rally)
- Candle closes in the lower half of the range
- Volume expanding but price making little progress (effort vs. reward divergence)

This is the first structural evidence that the uptrend is losing its buying conviction.

## Buying and Selling Climax

The climax is the explosive end of a trend — the "firework display":

- **Selling climax** — massive volume spike at the bottom of a downtrend. Panic selling meets aggressive buying from insiders. Often followed by a rapid bounce (automatic rally) then a test of the low on significantly lower volume.
- **Buying climax** — massive volume spike at the top of an uptrend. Euphoric public buying meets insiders distributing. Often followed by a sharp decline then a test of the high on significantly lower volume.

The test after the climax is the critical confirmation: if the retest is on low volume, the trend has exhausted.

## VPA in Practice

The daily routine of a VPA trader:

1. Scan charts for anomaly candles — price/volume disagreement
2. Identify the candle type (shooting star, hammer, doji, or neutral)
3. Assess volume relative to recent history (below avg, avg, above avg, climactic)
4. Look for confirmation: does the next candle validate or contradict the signal?
5. Reference the broader context: accumulation, distribution, or range?
