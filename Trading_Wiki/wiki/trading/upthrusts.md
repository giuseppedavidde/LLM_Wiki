# Upthrusts

> Sources: David Weis, 2013
> Raw: [Trades About to Happen](../../raw/trading/2026-05-20-trades-about-to-happen.md)

## Overview

An upthrust is a move above a previous line of resistance that fails to follow through and leads to a downward reversal. It is the mirror image of a spring. Upthrusts tend to be more difficult to trade than springs because the public is naturally inclined to buy breakouts and new highs.

## Key Characteristics

- Price rises above a well-defined resistance line
- The breakout fails to attract sufficient follow-through buying
- Price reverses downward, trapping breakout buyers
- The cumulative behavior before, during, and after the breakout tells the story

## Trend Context

- **In an uptrend**: upthrusts rarely pan out. Buying breakouts in a strong uptrend is generally a winning strategy.
- **In a downtrend**: upthrusts above a previous correction high have a greater probability of succeeding. They flourish in downtrends.

## Why They're Tricky

Professional selling at a top is often obscured under cover of public buying. Professionals need the public to buy so they can distribute their long positions and establish shorts. This makes upthrusts harder to identify in real time than springs, where professional buying is more visible.

## Trading Upthrusts

- Weis is not eager to buy breakouts precisely because of upthrust risk
- The first prerequisite is a previous resistance line
- An upthrust is a "potential" upthrust until confirmed by subsequent price action
- Entry: when price shows clear downward reversal after the resistance break
- Stop: near the extreme of the breakout
