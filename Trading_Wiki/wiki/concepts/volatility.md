## Definition

The magnitude and speed of price fluctuation, independent of direction. It is measured historically (HV — past realised) and by implication (IV — the option market's forward-looking consensus), and it is mean-reverting across regimes.

## How different sources approach it

- **Options Volatility (Overby):** HV is the 21–23 day annualised standard deviation; IV is solved from option prices via Black-Scholes. Volatility skew, the post-earnings "IV crush," and IV rank/percentile drive strategy selection — sell premium when IV is rich, buy when cheap.
- **Wyckoff 2.0 (Villahermosa):** Tick, volume, and range charts exploit different volatility regimes; the highest volatility and price movement occurs during specific sessions, and chart-type selection should adapt to it.
- **Crypto Technical Analysis (John & Law):** Crypto is "the most volatile major asset class," with single-day swings regularly exceeding 10%. Larger swings create clearer breakouts and more pronounced pattern completions, but also more false signals — confirmation is mandatory.
- **Understanding Price Action (Volman):** Volatility dictates spring reversal size (more volatile markets produce larger reversals) and stop width; when volatility contracts, attention shifts to 20-levels and tighter brackets rather than 00/50 stretches.
- **VPA (Coulling):** Bollinger Band squeezes (narrowing volatility) precede violent breakouts; effort-vs-reward divergence — large volume (effort) with narrow price spread (reward) — flags a volatility compression about to release.

## Cross-domain insights

Volatility is the common currency uniting the four domains. Options traders price it explicitly (vega, IV crush); crypto traders exploit its amplitude; scalpers adapt their tools to its regime; Wyckoff/VPA traders read its compression as the precursor to expansion. Everywhere, the structure is the same: volatility contracts, tension builds, then it releases.
