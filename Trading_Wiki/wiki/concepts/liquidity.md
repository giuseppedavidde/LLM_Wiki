## Definition

The ease with which an asset can be bought or sold without materially moving its price, determined by the depth of resting orders, the concentration of participants, and the size of the market. Liquidity governs slippage, fill quality, and how violently levels hold or fail.

## How different sources approach it

- **Market Ecosystem (Villahermosa):** Modern structure includes algorithmic/HFT trading, dark pools, and OTC venues that redistribute liquidity away from lit exchanges. Adaptive markets hypothesis reframes liquidity as dynamic and context-dependent rather than static.
- **Volume Profile (Dale/Villahermosa):** High Volume Nodes are zones of deep liquidity (acceptance); Low Volume Nodes are thin (rejection). Price moves fast through LVNs and tends to stall and rotate inside HVNs — liquidity literally maps the path of least resistance.
- **Crypto Technical Analysis (John & Law):** Lower market caps relative to equities mean support/resistance hold "more dramatically" as order flow concentrates at psychologically significant prices. Weekend sessions see thinner liquidity and more erratic moves, distorting formations.
- **Options Playbook (Overby):** Index options are recommended over single-stock options partly for "reduced volatility risk" via deeper liquidity; bid-ask spread quality determines whether a premium can actually be captured. Wide spreads make a strategy theoretical only.
- **VPA (Coulling) / Order Flow:** Tick and volume charts reveal liquidity entering in real time; delta divergence — price makes a new high but buying volume is lower — flags that liquidity is thinning behind the move.

## Cross-domain insights

Liquidity is the substrate every other concept sits on. Support/resistance "hold" because orders cluster there; breakouts fail when liquidity behind them is thin; options premiums depend on a tradeable bid-ask; crypto's exaggerated moves stem from shallower books. Read the liquidity, and the rest of the chart explains itself.
