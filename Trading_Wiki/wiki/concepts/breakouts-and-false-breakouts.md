## Definition

A breakout is price penetrating a trading-range boundary or key level with follow-through. A false breakout (or "false break") is the same penetration that fails to sustain, reversing to trap participants who entered on the break. Springs and upthrusts are the Wyckoff names for bullish and bearish false breakouts.

## How different sources approach it

- **Trades About to Happen (Weis):** The presence or absence of follow-through after a line penetration determines whether the break is real or a trap (spring/upthrust). A breakout is only a "potential" spring/upthrust until subsequent price action confirms the reversal.
- **Understanding Price Action (Volman):** Three break types — false break trap (no buildup, wide stop, skip), tease break (buildup but not at the barrier, risky), proper break (buildup directly at the level, tight stop). Buildup is mandatory; without it the offer is declined. False highs/lows at crucial S/R are major tells of the outcome.
- **VPA (Coulling):** A breakout on declining volume is a potential trap; a breakout becomes significant only when confirmed by expanding volume. Triangle/rectangle congestion that breaks on low volume is a trap.
- **Crypto Technical Analysis (John & Law):** High false-signal rate — crypto's volatility generates more "head fakes" than equities; confirmation through volume, multi-timeframe analysis, or secondary indicators is essential. Genuine moves show volume spikes coinciding with the breakthrough.
- **Wyckoff 2.0 (Villahermosa):** Springs at a Low Volume Node (rejection) with value-area support nearby and an upward-migrating POC are high-probability; Order Flow confirms via selling absorption → initiative buying → delta rotation → control+test.

## Cross-domain insights

The breakout/false-breakout dialectic is the single most repeated setup across every book. The consensus: never act on the break alone — require buildup (scalping), volume confirmation (crypto, VPA), or order-flow absorption (Wyckoff 2.0). The trap is the same everywhere; only the confirming tool changes by domain.
