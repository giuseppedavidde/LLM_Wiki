## Definition

The discipline of defining and capping loss on every trade through position sizing, stop placement, and defined-risk structures, so that no single outcome — or string of outcomes — can end a trading career.

## How different sources approach it

- **Options Playbook (Overby):** Every strategy entry lists max profit, max loss, breakeven, and Greek exposure. Defined-risk spreads cap the worst case; naked calls carry theoretically unlimited loss and are reserved for "All-Stars only."
- **Options Course Workbook (Fontanills):** "Risk is the price of the premium" — long options cap loss at premium paid, framing options as a leveraged substitute for stock with built-in stop-loss.
- **Understanding Price Action (Volman):** Stop placement is tied directly to buildup quality — a tighter, technically justified stop yields a comparable risk/reward at a worse (higher) entry. News-report, resistance, and reversal exits protect gains manually.
- **Crypto Technical Analysis (John & Law):** Strict stop-losses and appropriate position sizing are mandatory because crypto is "the most volatile major asset class"; high volatility means breakouts produce explosive moves both ways.
- **Scalping Low Volatility (Volman):** Tighter bracket and smaller position size adapt the standard framework when volatility contracts, preventing the same dollar risk from being spread across a wider range.

## Cross-domain insights

Every author, regardless of instrument, agrees loss must be defined *before* entry. Options traders buy defined-risk structures; scalpers tie stops to chart structure; crypto traders shrink size to absorb volatility. The mechanism differs — premium, technical stop, position size — but the principle is universal: survival precedes profitability.
