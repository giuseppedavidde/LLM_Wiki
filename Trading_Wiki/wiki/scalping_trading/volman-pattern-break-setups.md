# Volman Pattern Break Setups

> Sources: Bob Volman, 2014
> Raw: [Understanding Price Action](../../raw/scalping-trading/2026-05-20-understanding-price-action.md)

## Overview

Volman defines four trade setups for the 5-minute frame, all sharing three core requirements: trade in line with dominance, take position on a break away from buildup, and find a specific development within the buildup that sets up the entry. The standard bracket is a 10-pip stop and 20-pip target.

## Pattern Break

A straightforward breakout from a buildup cluster at a key boundary. The setup unfolds in four steps: (1) aggressors attack the boundary, (2) defenders fend off the initial charge, (3) a cluster of alternating bars builds tension (buildup/squeeze), and (4) prices break out and follow through via double pressure.

**Signal bar**: the bar at the end of the buildup cluster, closing strongly against the pattern barrier (bullish bar for a long, bearish bar for a short). **Entry**: taken the moment a subsequent bar (entry bar) surpasses the signal bar's high/low by one pip.

**Stop**: placed above/below the buildup cluster. **Target**: 20 pips (standard bracket).

Key checks before entry:
- Dominant pressure alignment
- No major obstacles overhead/underfoot
- Favorable round number magnet ~20 pips out
- Adequate buildup (cluster should be visible, preferably 4+ bars for range breakouts)

![Pattern break from a squeeze progression](images/volman_p88_1.jpeg)

Trading off a signal bar that is sticking out of the pattern is acceptable, but the bar should not be overly tall. Preferable to have the stop reside inside the pattern so a pullback to test the broken barrier does not immediately shake out the trade.

## Pattern Break Pullback

When the initial break is skipped (too little buildup, uncomfortable signal bar, etc.), the post-breakout action may still offer a second-instance entry. Two scenarios:

1. **Stalling outside the barrier** — prices cling around the broken level after the break, building up tension for another leg in line with the original break.
2. **Pullback to the barrier** — prices follow through initially, then pull back to test the broken level (now acting as S/R flip). The pullback should be orderly/diagonal, not aggressive.

The signal bar sets up in the area of the broken barrier, preferably coinciding with the 25ema and a round number (triple support). Entry is taken on the break of this bar in the direction of the original breakout.

![Pattern break pullback with ceiling test](images/volman_p108_1.jpeg)

Better R:R than the initial break because the stop can be tighter. Highly preferable for the signal bar to set up not only at the broken barrier but also at the 25ema.

## Pattern Break Combi

A two-bar pattern consisting of a **powerbar** (tall bar showing directional pressure) followed by an **inside bar** (bar that does not exceed the powerbar's high or low). The inside bar functions as the signal bar. The combi is most potent when part of a bigger breakout pattern — hence the name.

Variants:
- Standard combi: powerbar + inside bar
- Reversed combi: inside bar + powerbar (equally valid)
- Three-bar combi: powerbar + two inside bars (adds tension)
- Double-bar break: both bars share the same high/low at the breaking end

Entry is taken on the break of the inside bar. The inside bar's body color should preferably match the powerbar's, but a neutral doji or opposite color is acceptable if positioned favorably.

![Combi setup examples](images/volman_p119_1.jpeg)

A combi by itself is never a reason to trade — it is merely a tool to time entry in a breakout situation. The surrounding conditions (dominance, buildup, absence of adverse magnets) determine validity.

## Pullback Reversal

Trading the reversal of a corrective swing against the dominant trend (or within a range). Pullbacks come in two varieties: **correction in price** (diagonal) and **correction in time** (horizontal flag).

Guidelines for price corrections:
- Should retrace 40–60% of the dominant swing (50% is ideal)
- Should reach or slightly pierce the 25ema
- Preferably puts in a technical test (S/R level, ceiling test, former high/low)
- First pullback after a breakout is generally the most eagerly countered

Entry: taken on the break of a signal bar in the turn area. The turn often shows at least a few bars of stalling/buildup before the pullback is ready to roll over.

**Harmony principle**: the pullback's bars should not exceed the average span of the dominant swing bars. The best candidate looks "reluctant" against the dominant pressure.

![Pullback reversal with buildup at 25ema](images/volman_p136_1.jpeg)

For corrections in time (flags), treat the reversal as a regular pattern break setup. The 25ema filter alone prevents the majority of pullback reversal traps.
