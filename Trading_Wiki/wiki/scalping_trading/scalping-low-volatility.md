## Overview

When the Forex markets enter a persistent low-volatility environment (as seen in 2014 with record-low intraday ranges), Volman adapts the standard 5-minute methodology rather than abandoning it. Price action principles remain unchanged — only the strategic procedure is tweaked. The key changes: tighter bracket settings, use of a faster tick chart (200-tick), and shifting focus from 00/50-levels to 20-levels.

## Adapting the Bracket

In low volatility, the standard 20-pip target becomes unrealistic. Suggested adjustments:

- Target: **8–12 pips** (instead of 20)
- Stop: **6–10 pips** (tighter protection)
- Exits best executed **discretionarily** within this range, especially on the stop side
- Always enter with a preset stop even if target is left open (protects against platform crash or connection failure)

The risk/reward ratio should still favor the trade, but absolute pip expectations shrink.

## Switching to a Faster Frame

Volman recommends switching from the 5-minute time frame to a **200-tick chart**. Benefits:

- Prints bars only when there is trading activity — stale phases produce fewer useless bars
- Resembles a 2-3 minute frame in active phases but compresses dead periods
- Buildup clusters and boundaries line up just as neatly, often more so
- Allows tighter technical stops

A tick-counter in the chart corner helps track progress within the current bar.

## Round Number Adjustments

Replace the thinly plotted 00 and 50-levels with **20-levels** (00, 20, 40, 60, 80). In low volatility, prices relentlessly back-and-fill between two 20-levels rather than marching between 00 and 50. Always pay extra attention between the 40 and 60-levels.

## Entry Criteria Tweaks

- **Box progressions** become the primary setup — lengthy box ranges with a squeeze against the barrier offer the best follow-through
- Avoid quick continuation scalps in the highs/lows of the session — stick to breakouts from the 25ema base
- Require **more buildup** than on the 5-minute frame; a tease break in low volatility is even more dangerous
- **First breaks** of a round number are less likely to follow through — anticipate resistance and wait for second-instance entries
- The pole-flag-swing principle still applies but targets should be conservative

## Non-Forex Markets

Volman demonstrates the adaptability of his method on popular futures markets using tick charts:

| Market | Tick Setting | Round Numbers |
|--------|-------------|---------------|
| S&P 500 e-mini | 2000-tick | 5 and 10-point levels |
| Nasdaq 100 e-mini | 200-tick | 5 and 10-levels |
| Dow mini | 400-tick | 00 and 50-levels (in the 17,000 range) |

Price action mechanics on futures are virtually identical to Forex. Key considerations:

- **After-hours trading** (post 16:00 EST) is very thin — few bars on tick charts despite many clock minutes
- **US Open at 9:30 EST** often prints tall fickle bars — faster frames (tick charts) cut these to tradable size
- **Cross-market correlation** exists (e.g., EU/UK stock market opens affect US futures) but is irrelevant — focus on the chart
- Round number levels and pattern lines work the same way
