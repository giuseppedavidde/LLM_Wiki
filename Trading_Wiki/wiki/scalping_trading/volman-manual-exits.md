# Volman Manual Exits

> Sources: Bob Volman, 2014
> Raw: [Understanding Price Action](../../raw/scalping-trading/2026-05-20-understanding-price-action.md)

## Overview

The standard 20/10 bracket is highly effective and removes emotional interference. However, discretionary intervention can improve results in specific situations. Three manual exit techniques are covered: news report exit, resistance exit, and reversal exit. These require suppressing fear-based instincts and exiting solely on technical grounds.

## News Report Exit

The 5-minute chart becomes unreliable during scheduled news releases. Major reports (NFP, FOMC, ECB, interest rate decisions, central bank speeches) can produce 50+ pip spikes with severe slippage on stop orders.

Three options when a news report approaches while in position: (a) stay in and take chances, (b) reduce exposure, (c) get out of the way entirely. Volman's definite advice: shun the news and get out.

Key points:
- **Slippage risk**: stop orders on the bracket are market orders; during news, slippage of 10+ pips beyond the stop is not unheard of.
- **Pre-news flatness**: when prices go flat for hours before a release, this often signals big parties laying low — the response on release is typically vicious.
- **Buildup breaks before news**: highly poisonous to play.
- **Never hold intraday positions over the weekend**.

Nonfarm payrolls, housing/production reports, job/unemployment data, and interest rate decisions are the highest-impact events for EUR/USD and USD crosses. The most dangerous time is 14:30 CET (one hour before US stock market open).

## Resistance Exit

Exit at an obvious S/R level before price has a chance to reverse off it. The reduced target should be determined **before entry**, not as a sudden bailout decision.

- Minimum acceptable profit: ~14 pips (maintaining at least a 1:1 risk/reward)
- Apply selectively: more caution in ranging markets near highs/lows; less need in trending markets where resistance tends to get taken out
- Typical S/R levels to target: well-defined range barriers, round numbers, double tops/bottoms, pattern line extensions, former swing highs/lows

![Resistance exit candidates in a ranging session](images/volman_p160_1.jpeg)

> "The aim should be to exit in strength rather than weakness. Hence the resistance exit."

Avoid the trap of scratching at break-even on the first pullback — this is equivalent to being stopped out twice on a 10-pip stop.

## Reversal Exit

Exit when the price action builds up against the open position and shows signs of reversing. The most common reversal formation is the **M-pattern** (for a long exit) or **W-pattern** (for a short exit).

Structure of a reversal exit signal:
1. Left leg (in the direction of the trade)
2. Middle-part (buildup/cluster that stalls the move)
3. A powerbar break of the middle-part in the *opposite* direction

Entry is not required — the exit is triggered by the break of the adverse powerbar (or combi/inside bar) within the middle-part.

Key distinction: a sideways cluster that breaks *against* the trade after solid follow-through is much more alarming than a pullback that merely retraces diagonally. The more rebellious the sideways action, the stronger the reversal signal.

**Evening-star pattern** (three-bar reversal: powerbar up, small doji, powerbar down) and **morning-star pattern** (mirror image) are potent reversal exit triggers.

![Evening-star and M-pattern reversal exit examples](images/volman_p168_1.jpeg)

The reversal exit is usually deployed with minimal damage or even a small profit, since the trade was already in profit before the reversal built up. If adverse pressure appears immediately after entry (before any profit), it is better to let the bracket stop handle it than to try discretionary reversal exits.
