# Volman Price Action Principles

> Sources: Bob Volman, 2014
> Raw: [Understanding Price Action](../../raw/scalping-trading/2026-05-20-understanding-price-action.md)

## Overview

Bob Volman's price action method is built on seven core principles that repeat across any time frame and market. These principles form the foundation for a breakout-based scalping methodology on the 5-minute chart. The core idea: identify the party in control, wait for buildup (pre-breakout tension) at a key level, and enter on the break of a crucial bar.

## Double Pressure

For prices to follow through in substantial fashion, assistance is needed from both sides of the market. A bull move needs not only bulls buying in, but bears forced to cover their shorts. When both camps temporarily join forces on the same side, this is **double pressure**. It produces follow-through — sustained directional movement.

The most promising way to exploit double pressure is to anticipate its origin — to detect the "sweetspots" in the chart where the market has built up to a boiling point. This pre-breakout tension (buildup) manifests as a tight cluster of alternating bars at a boundary, where attackers and defenders fight for control. The longer the tug-of-war, the more parties trapped on the wrong side when prices finally break, adding to the breakout momentum.

![Buildup clusters preceding double-pressure pops](images/volman_p18_1.jpeg)

## Support and Resistance

Former support, once broken, tends to become resistance (role reversal). Former resistance becomes support. Volman warns against aggressive contrarian plays — shorting former highs or buying former lows without confirmation. The conservative practice is to monitor how the market handles itself *in and around* these levels before acting.

The biggest virtue of S/R levels is that they reveal the **dominant party**. The most effective way to identify dominance is to follow the overall slope of the market. When bulls are in control, prices make higher highs and bearish corrections struggle to surpass former lows. It takes time for a market to roll over — the stronger the earlier dominance, the less likely a first reversal attempt will succeed.

![It takes time for a market to roll over — break at point c has better odds than point a](images/volman_p20_1.jpeg)

> "We shouldn't trade against dominance."

## False Breaks, Tease Breaks, and Proper Breaks

There are three ways for the market to execute a break, regardless of alignment with dominance:

1. **False break trap** — no buildup prior to the break. Requires a wide stop. Highly attractive to contrarians. Best skipped.
2. **Tease break** — some buildup, but not directly at the barrier. May follow through but often not straightaway. Risky with a tight stop.
3. **Proper break** — solid buildup directly at the breakout level. Tight stop can be placed above/below the buildup cluster. The most favorable scenario.

Three conditions demand examination in all situations:
- (a) Is the break in line with dominant pressure or against it?
- (b) Is the market ranging or trending?
- (c) Are there obstacles overhead/underfoot?

Buildup is **mandatory**. Without it, the offer is best declined.

![Difference in buildup: false break trap, tease break, proper break](images/volman_p24_1.jpeg)

Where a stop can be placed is directly tied to the quality of buildup. The wider the stop needed for technical justification, the less attractive the breakout from a continuation perspective.

## False Highs and Lows

A **false high** occurs when a bull break is followed by a bear break that takes out the breakout bar at the bottom. A **false low** is the mirror image. At a crucial spot — particularly within a buildup cluster — a false high/low can be a major tell of the likely outcome.

Key principles:
- A false break at the end of a swing *in line* with dominant pressure → likely temporary correction.
- A false break at the end of a *correction* → dominant pressure likely to resurface.
- A buildup break that fails anyway → serious power shift ahead.

![False highs and lows announcing reversals](images/volman_p28_1.jpeg)

False highs/lows gain relevance at crucial S/R levels. A false low in support will not go unnoticed; a false high in resistance signals waning momentum.

## Pullback Reversals

Two popular strategies for timing pullback reversals:

1. **Percentage retracement** — measure the dominant move, wait for a 40–60% retracement.
2. **Technical test** — wait for the pullback to reach a former level of S/R within the trending swing.

The conservative route: **wait for stalling/buildup** in the reversal area before entering. Do not buy or sell straight into a retracement, even at a technical test. The extra bars of sideways action build required tension, offer a better view of the break level, and allow a tighter stop.

![Pullback reversal with buildup at a key level](images/volman_p33_1.jpeg)

A higher entry with buildup may compare favorably to a lower entry without it. The tighter stop on the conservative entry can yield a similar risk/reward ratio.

## Ceiling Test

A **ceiling** is the low of a topping progression (or the high of a bottoming progression). When a pullback comes to test this mini barrier from below (or above), this is a ceiling test — a highly anticipated event.

The ceiling test explains why tease breakouts are premature: the market needs to first retrace to the nearest level of former support (the ceiling) before bouncing into a more powerful breakout. The presence or absence of a ceiling test can determine whether a subsequent break is worth accepting.

![Ceiling test at work in a ranging market](images/volman_p37_1.jpeg)

The magnet effect: a ceiling level acts as a magnet for pullbacks. Rather than turning around at the most obvious S/R level, prices often gun for the *last* level of former support, filling the "hollow space" between the range barrier and the ceiling test extension.

## Round Number Effect

On the EUR/USD 5-minute, the key round numbers are the **00 and 50-levels** (full and half cents). These levels act as powerful magnets. Prices tend to work up to and revolve around them for hours.

- **Favorable magnet**: a round number ~20 pips out from entry, pulling prices toward target.
- **Adverse magnet**: a round number too close to entry or stop, threatening to pull prices against the trade.

A primary goal is to set up trades in line with a favorable magnet while avoiding the adverse pull of a 00/50-level. In low-volatility environments, attention shifts from 00/50 stretches to the **20-levels**.
