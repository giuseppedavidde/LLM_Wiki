## Path

A path through the central temporal disagreement in the wiki: whether time in a trade is profit (theta seller), cost (Volman scalper), or cause (Wyckoff structuralist) — and how each philosophy's survival rule converges on the same commandment.

1. Start with: [[volman-price-action-principles|The scalper's seven principles — double pressure, buildup, false/proper breaks]]
2. Then understand: [[volman-pattern-break-setups|The immediacy — the trade fires on the break of a single 5-minute bar]]
3. Adapt the regime: [[scalping-low-volatility|Drop to a 200-tick chart to wipe out the dead time]]
4. Meet the patient structuralist: [[wyckoff-method-overview|Wyckoff — weeks-long accumulation/distribution cause before any effect]]
5. Challenge with: [[wyckoff-structural-patience-vs-scalping-breakout-immediacy|The contradiction — patience vs decisiveness as a personal scarcity]]
6. Add the options angle: [[theta-premium-selling-vs-buildup-waiting|Time as profit (theta) vs time as cost (buildup)]]
7. End at: [[risk-management|The universal survival rule — define the loss before entry, in either philosophy]]

## Why this trail

Time is the variable every author fights over but no two frame the same way. Volman bleeds while waiting, the theta seller profits while waiting, and Wyckoff *requires* waiting as the precondition of a large move. The trail forces those three relationships to collide at steps 4–6 so that by step 7 the reader sees the only point of agreement: **loss must be defined before entry** — by premium, by structural stop, or by position size. The intermediate contradiction and theta stops are staged to dispel the illusion that "patience" and "discipline" are synonyms; in this wiki they are often opposites, and the trader's job is to know which vehicle they are driving before they choose which virtue to practise.
