### Foreword (by Dr. Alexander Elder)
David Weis expands on the classical works of Richard Wyckoff, building a modern superstructure of market analysis. The changing heights of price bars accompanied by rising or falling volume are the basic irreducible elements. Weis uses these patterns to read crowd behavior across all markets and timeframes.

### Introduction
Weis's approach incorporates price range, close, and volume, utilizing what he calls "the story of the lines" — the price/volume behavior framed and interconnected by lines drawn on charts. Key elements of the studies throughout the book:
- Compare effort of buying/selling with reward (volume vs. progress)
- Watch for ease of movement or lack of movement (wide vs. narrow price bars)
- Consider the meaning of the close within the range of a price bar
- Watch for shortening of upward or downward thrust
- Watch for follow-through or lack of follow-through after penetrations of support/resistance (springs and upthrusts)
- Watch for tests of high-volume areas
- Consider interaction of price with trend lines, channels, and support/resistance lines

### Chapter 1: Where to Find Trades (An Overview)
Big trades appear with greater frequency around the edges of trading ranges. Trading ranges are rectangular-shaped with prices swinging between upper and lower boundaries, or coiling into apexes. The boundaries are repeatedly tested as buyers and sellers struggle for dominance. After breakouts or breakdowns, prices often retest these areas. The behavior described occurs on all charts regardless of time period.

### Chapter 2: Drawing Lines
Drawing support and resistance lines frames the struggle between buyers and sellers. Lines bring the price/volume story into focus. Trading ranges within larger ranges are common. By drawing lines, the trader can anticipate price swings to peak or bottom around previous support/resistance lines. Axis lines (where prices revolve around a horizontal line) are particularly useful on daily charts.

### Chapter 3: The Story of the Lines
Lines interconnect to tell a story. The interaction of price with previously drawn lines reveals the behavior within the range. Trend lines, channels, and support/resistance lines highlight potential turning points.

### Chapter 4: The Logic of Reading Bar Charts
Each price bar has three components: price range, position of the close within that range, and volume. The relationship between these three components reveals the struggle between buyers and sellers. Key concepts:
- Wide range bars with closes near the high show strength; near the low show weakness
- Narrow range bars indicate indecision or balance
- Volume confirms or contradicts the price action
- Tests of high-volume areas often mark turning points

### Chapter 5: Springs
A spring is a washout (penetration) of a trading range or support level that fails to follow through and leads to an upward reversal. Key points:
- Springs in an uptrend have higher success rates
- The lack of follow-through after a support break raises the possibility of a spring (not a guarantee)
- Market volatility often dictates the size of a spring's upward reversal
- The amount of preparation (trading range size) determines the magnitude of the resulting up-move
- Engineered sell-offs below support test whether supply exists; if not, large operators buy aggressively

### Chapter 6: Upthrusts
An upthrust is a failed breakout above resistance that leads to a downward reversal. Key points:
- Upthrusts are more difficult to trade than springs because public likes to buy new highs
- In an uptrend, upthrusts rarely pan out; in a downtrend, they have greater probability
- The cumulative behavior on preceding and succeeding price bars tells the story
- A move above resistance is a "potential" upthrust until confirmed by subsequent action

### Chapter 7: Absorption
Absorption is the process through which long liquidation, profit taking, and new short selling are overcome. Clues pointing to successful absorption:
- Rising supports
- Volume increases around the top of the absorption area
- Lack of downward follow-through after threatening price bars
- Prices pressing against resistance without giving ground
- Absorption areas are generally shallow when viewed as corrections
- Can occur at tops (buyers overcoming sellers) or bottoms (sellers overcoming buyers)

### Chapter 8: Chart Studies
Application of all previous concepts to real charts across stocks, futures, and forex. Includes analysis of forex tick volume and true range.

Key concepts: shortening of thrust, ease of movement, stopping volume, and interaction with trend lines.
